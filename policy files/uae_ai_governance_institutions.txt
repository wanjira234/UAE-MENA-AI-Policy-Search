UAE AI Governance Institutions and the National AI Security Policy
=======================================================================

Institutional landscape
--------------------------
UAE AI governance is spread across several bodies rather than concentrated in
a single regulator. The Minister of State for Artificial Intelligence holds
overall policy responsibility and chairs coordination with the Emirates
Council for Artificial Intelligence and Digital Transactions, which aligns
federal and emirate-level implementation. Data protection sits separately
with the UAE Data Office, created under Federal Decree-Law No. 44 of 2021.
Security-specific oversight of AI systems is led by the Cyber Security
Council, which issued the National Artificial Intelligence Security Policy in
2025.

International policy framework
----------------------------------
In September 2024, the UAE Cabinet issued an international policy on AI that
builds on the country's earlier AI charter and sets out a strategic framework
for pursuing the UAE's AI targets while following internationally recognised
best practices. As part of this framework, the UAE introduced a "Seal of
Approval" intended to recognise AI companies that meet high standards of
quality and ethics, giving the government a way to reward responsible
development rather than relying solely on penalties for non-compliance.

The National AI Security Policy
-----------------------------------
The Cyber Security Council's 2025 policy complements the National AI Strategy
2031 by focusing specifically on the secure, controlled, and trustworthy
deployment of AI systems. Its core themes are risk management, mandatory
human oversight for consequential decisions, and protection against misuse of
AI capabilities. Where the National AI Strategy sets ambition and the PDPL
governs personal data, this policy is the instrument most directly concerned
with AI system security posture: how models are deployed, monitored, and
constrained against adversarial or unintended use.

Funding and infrastructure backing
---------------------------------------
Abu Dhabi's sovereign wealth funds, Mubadala and ADQ, play a direct role in
translating policy into infrastructure. Both funds finance large-scale AI
compute and data infrastructure, establish or back AI-focused companies and
research funds, and form partnerships with established international AI
players. This sovereign-capital backing is one of the more distinctive
features of the UAE's approach compared with jurisdictions where AI policy is
purely regulatory: the government is simultaneously the rule-setter and one
of the largest capital allocators in the domestic AI ecosystem, including
through the US-UAE AI Acceleration Partnership aimed at building AI
infrastructure with US public and private partners.

How the pieces fit together
--------------------------------
A useful way to separate these instruments: the National AI Strategy 2031 sets
the destination and economic targets; the PDPL governs how personal data may
be used, including in AI training and inference; the National AI Security
Policy governs how AI systems themselves must be secured and overseen once
deployed; and the Seal of Approval and sovereign fund activity are the
incentive and capital mechanisms meant to pull the ecosystem toward that
destination faster than regulation alone would achieve.
