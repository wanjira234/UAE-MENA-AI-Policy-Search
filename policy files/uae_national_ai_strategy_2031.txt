UAE National Strategy for Artificial Intelligence 2031
=========================================================

Origin and scope
-----------------
Announced by the UAE Cabinet in October 2017 and formally adopted in 2019, this
strategy is the country's overarching framework for becoming a global hub for
artificial intelligence by 2031. It sits inside the broader UAE Centennial 2071
vision and is overseen by the Minister of State for Artificial Intelligence, a
role the UAE created as the first of its kind in the world. Implementation is
coordinated through the Emirates Council for Artificial Intelligence and Digital
Transactions, which keeps federal and emirate-level bodies moving in step with
one another.

Strategic objectives
---------------------
The strategy lays out eight objectives: build a reputation as an AI destination,
strengthen the UAE's competitive position in priority sectors through AI
deployment, grow a fertile domestic AI ecosystem, embed AI across government
services to improve citizens' daily lives, attract and train AI talent, bring
world-leading research capability into target industries, supply the data and
infrastructure needed to make the UAE a testbed for AI, and put in place strong
governance and effective regulation.

Talent and research
--------------------
Workforce development is treated as central to the whole effort. Plans include
public AI literacy campaigns, specialised upskilling for STEM graduates and
government staff, and partnerships with international experts. Two flagship
mechanisms support this: a National Virtual AI Institute meant to pool global
and local research expertise, and a Key Thinkers Program that brings leading
researchers into direct collaboration with UAE institutions. An open-access AI
library is also planned to make accumulated knowledge broadly available rather
than siloed inside individual agencies.

Sector deployment and targets
------------------------------
AI adoption is targeted at concrete societal problems such as healthcare
improvement and traffic safety, partly through national AI challenges that
invite solutions to specific problems. On the economic side, the strategy aims
to lift AI's contribution to GDP from roughly 9 percent toward 45 percent by
2031, a shift estimated to add about AED 335 billion in economic value. Related
targets include training 10,000 UAE-based data scientists and machine learning
engineers, supported by golden visa pathways for international experts, and
moving public services toward a fully paper-free standard across all emirates.

Governance and accountability
-------------------------------
The strategy commits to annual AI Value Reports that track outcomes such as
jobs created, patents filed, and carbon savings, intended to keep the roadmap
accountable rather than aspirational. It also anticipates that AI agents will
increasingly carry out operational government functions directly, such as
screening visa applications or pre-clearing shipping manifests, which raises
the stakes for the governance and oversight mechanisms described elsewhere in
UAE policy, particularly the National AI Security Policy and the Personal Data
Protection Law.

Relationship to other instruments
------------------------------------
This strategy is the umbrella document; it does not itself set out data
protection rules or AI-specific security requirements. Those are handled by
separate instruments, namely Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (the Personal
Data Protection Law) and the National Artificial Intelligence Security Policy
issued by the Cyber Security Council. Readers looking for compliance detail
should consult those documents rather than this strategic overview.
