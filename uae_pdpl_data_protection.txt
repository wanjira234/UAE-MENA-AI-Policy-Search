UAE Personal Data Protection Law (Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021)
========================================================================

Background
-----------
The UAE Cabinet enacted Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 in November 2021,
giving the country its first comprehensive federal data protection law. It
took effect on 2 January 2022 following a six-month grace period for
organisations to reach compliance. The law is commonly referred to as the
PDPL. A companion law, Federal Decree-Law No. 44 of 2021, established the UAE
Data Office as the dedicated regulator responsible for proposing data
protection policy, approving compliance standards, handling complaints, and
issuing implementation guidance.

Scope and applicability
-------------------------
The PDPL applies to any data controller or processor located in the UAE that
processes personal data, regardless of whether the individuals concerned are
inside or outside the country. It also reaches organisations established
outside the UAE if they process personal data belonging to UAE residents,
giving it an extraterritorial reach similar in spirit to the EU's GDPR. Several
categories sit outside its scope: government data, public-sector entities,
purely personal use of data, health and credit data (which are governed by
their own sector-specific laws), and entities operating in free zones that
already maintain their own data protection regimes, such as the Dubai
International Financial Centre.

Core obligations
------------------
Organisations subject to the PDPL must establish a valid legal basis before
processing personal data, put in place appropriate technical and
organisational safeguards, appoint a Data Protection Officer where the volume
or sensitivity of processing warrants it, and carry out Data Protection Impact
Assessments for higher-risk processing activities. The law also sets
conditions, rather than an outright ban, on transferring personal data outside
the UAE, with transfers permitted to jurisdictions the UAE Data Office
recognises as offering adequate protection.

Rights for individuals
-------------------------
Data subjects gain a defined set of rights under the law, including the right
to be informed about how their data is processed and a right relevant
specifically to automated decision-making: individuals may object to being
subject to a decision based solely on automated processing, including
AI-driven decisions, where that decision produces legal or similarly
significant effects. This provision is one of the clearest points of contact
between the PDPL and AI governance more broadly, since it constrains how
automated systems can be deployed against UAE residents without human
oversight.

Enforcement
-------------
The UAE Data Office has moved relatively quickly on enforcement and guidance
compared with the slower early years some other jurisdictions experienced
after passing similar laws. Administrative fines apply for violations, with
exact amounts left to executive regulations; violations can also expose
organisations to civil liability and regulatory inspections. Practitioners
note that GDPR compliance does not automatically satisfy PDPL requirements,
particularly around cross-border transfer rules, since adequacy is determined
by the UAE Data Office rather than by reference to EU adequacy findings.

Relevance to AI deployments
------------------------------
For organisations deploying AI systems in the UAE, the PDPL's most consequential
provisions concern consent for data used in training or inference, data
residency requirements that can affect where models and pipelines may run,
and the right to an explanation when automated decisions carry significant
consequences. The PDPL itself is not an AI-specific law; AI-focused security
expectations are instead set out separately in the National Artificial
Intelligence Security Policy of 2025.
