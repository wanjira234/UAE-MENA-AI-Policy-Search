Saudi Arabia's National Strategy for Data and AI (NSDAI) and SDAIA
========================================================================

Institutional structure
---------------------------
The Saudi Data and Artificial Intelligence Authority, established by royal
decree in 2019, is the Kingdom's central body for both data governance and AI
strategy. SDAIA operates through two principal subsidiaries: the National
Data Management Office, which handles data governance and regulation, and the
National Center for Artificial Intelligence, which leads AI research,
development, and adoption. A third unit, the National Information Center,
supports the broader mandate. This dual structure lets SDAIA pursue
regulatory and promotional goals at the same time, rather than splitting them
across separate agencies the way some other jurisdictions do.

The National Strategy for Data and AI
-----------------------------------------
SDAIA announced the National Strategy for Data and Artificial Intelligence
(NSDAI) in October 2020 at the first Global AI Summit in Riyadh, under the
banner "Where the best of Data and AI is made reality." The strategy aims to
position Saudi Arabia as a global leader in data and AI by 2030, using a
phased approach: addressing national urgencies first, then building
longer-term competitive foundations. Measurable targets cover investment
raised, the number of trained data and AI specialists, the number of AI
startups, and the Kingdom's standing in open-data and research-publication
rankings.

Data governance framework
-----------------------------
SDAIA has built a national data governance framework covering collection,
storage, sharing, quality, privacy, and security standards across both
government and private-sector entities. This framework is designed to make
high-quality data available for AI development while still protecting
individual privacy, and it works alongside Saudi Arabia's Personal Data
Protection Law, which SDAIA helped develop and which draws on international
best practice while accommodating the Kingdom's specific legal context.

Monitoring and accountability
---------------------------------
Progress against NSDAI targets is tracked through an AI readiness index for
government entities and dashboards published on the National Data Governance
Platform. SDAIA conducts periodic reviews and publishes public reporting
intended to support international benchmarking, signalling that the strategy
is meant to be evaluated against external as well as internal standards.

Risk-based AI regulation
----------------------------
SDAIA has released a draft National AI Risk Management Framework that
introduces a risk-based classification system distinguishing low-risk from
high-risk AI applications, each carrying different compliance expectations
for developers and deployers. As of the most recent guidance, Saudi Arabia
does not yet have a single standalone law covering all AI systems; instead,
AI-relevant obligations are distributed across the PDPL, the NSDAI's
governance framework, and this emerging risk-classification framework, with
observers expecting consolidation toward a dedicated AI law over time.

Relationship to commercial AI development
----------------------------------------------
A 2025 institutional shift split Saudi Arabia's AI development across two
tracks: SDAIA and NCAI retain research authority, while HUMAIN, a
state-backed company launched under the Public Investment Fund, was
positioned as the production and commercial distribution channel for advanced
Saudi AI models, including the Arabic-language model ALLaM. This split
mirrors how some other countries separate state research institutes from
commercial champions, and 2026 was designated the Kingdom's "Year of
Artificial Intelligence" to mark the shift from strategy to execution.
